Who this recitation is for

The recitation is optional, informal, and usually 5-15 people. It is built for the students actually taking this course, not for a grade.

  • Most attendees are from the Data Science major, and most are sophomores and juniors.
  • Most of you already code in Python and Java.
  • You have basic knowledge of algorithms and abstract data structures — lists, stacks, queues, trees, heaps and hash tables.
  • Some of you have experience with other languages; bring them if you like.
No grade, no submission

Nothing in the recitation is submitted or graded. Come for all of it or drop in for the part you need.

What we do in a session

  1. Concept review (15–20 min). Use the notes page to review sorting ideas, stability, and complexity.
  2. Practice (25-30 min). Choose from the exercises and work at your own pace. There is no expectation to finish all six. Problem 6 is optional exploration and is not covered in class.
  3. Discuss approaches (15 min). Review selected problems through their key ideas, correctness, and complexity, without a line-by-line code walkthrough.
  4. Questions (remaining time). Bring your attempts, homework questions, or anything from lecture you want to revisit.

Sample solutions are provided in C, C++, C#, Java, Python and JavaScript. Other languages are welcome, but are not covered by the sample solutions.

Recommended IDEs

Either editor is a good choice for the recitation. Choose based on whether you want multi-language flexibility or a Python-focused workspace.

Visual Studio Code (General Choice)

A lightweight, customizable text editor with extensions for Python, Java, C/C++, C#, and JavaScript. Jupyter Notebooks are also supported via plugins. Recommended if you want one editor for every language used in the recitation. Free and Open Source.

Download VS Code

PyCharm (Heavier but Stronger)

A full-stack, Python-focused IDE with code completion, a visual debugger, refactoring tools, and notebook support. Recommended if you want more guidance while writing and debugging Python. Activate education license with your School email to use it freely.

Download PyCharm

AI policy

Allowed, with understanding

AI tools are fully allowed during the recitation. However, you should understand the solution and be able to explain it in your own words. If you cannot explain a line, treat that as a sign to slow down or ask.

This permission applies to recitation practice. Follow the instructor's rules for assignments and exams; those rules are not changed by this policy.
