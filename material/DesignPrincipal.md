# Design Principles for Recitation Materials

- Recitation supports the course's learning objectives through guided practice,
  discussion, and feedback. Materials should help students consolidate lecture
  content, identify misconceptions, and explain their reasoning, rather than serve
  as a general-purpose reference or tutorial.
- Recitation takes place on Tuesday, while the teaching week runs from Monday
  through Thursday. Plan each session around a rolling window: the latter half of
  the previous week's lectures and the current week's early lectures delivered
  before recitation. Follow actual lecture progress, rather than treating each
  syllabus week as a single recitation unit. Core exercises must not assume topics
  scheduled for later in the current week; carry those topics into the following
  Tuesday's session. For the first session, use only material already introduced
  and any necessary prerequisite review.
- Keep materials concise and focused. Choose explanations, examples, and exercises
  for the specific learning goals of the session; avoid unnecessary complexity and
  tangential content.
- Across each exercise set, include both conceptual reasoning and implementation
  or application. Suitable formats include written explanations, counterexamples,
  algorithm traces, diagrams, debugging, coding, and SQL queries. Individual
  questions do not need to combine every type of activity.
- Conceptual questions should ask students to explain why an approach works,
  compare alternatives, identify assumptions, or diagnose mistakes, rather than
  simply recall definitions or reproduce memorized steps.
- Match difficulty to students' prior knowledge and the available practice time.
  Students should be able to make meaningful progress using concepts already
  introduced or explicitly scaffolded in the materials. LeetCode Easy–Medium may
  serve as a rough reference for algorithm implementation questions, but is not a
  requirement or a difficulty scale for other types of exercises.
- Let learning goals determine the mix of activities; do not require equal numbers
  or equal difficulty of written and coding questions. Provide a core exercise path
  designed for approximately 30 minutes of in-class practice. This excludes concept
  review, discussion, and questions, and does not require students to complete the
  entire exercise set. Label additional practice and challenge questions clearly.
- Provide a feedback path through staged hints, self-checks, reference explanations,
  or discussion. Answers should explain the reasoning, not just present a result or
  code. Include correctness and complexity where relevant, or explain database
  results, constraints, and transaction behavior as appropriate.
- Keep core practice aligned with the syllabus. Extra content is optional enrichment
  provided by the TA, is not covered in the final exam, and must not be an implicit
  prerequisite for core exercises.
- AI tools are allowed during recitation under the recitation policy. Regardless of
  the tools used, students should be able to explain, verify, and modify their
  answers. Assignment and exam policies remain those set by the instructor.
